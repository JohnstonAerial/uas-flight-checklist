## Find your way around

The app has three tabs at the top.

- **🌤 Conditions** is where it opens: the preflight weather card, the airspace and TFR buttons, and the place you're checking.
- **✅ Checklist** is your checklist. The General, Mapping and Commercial tabs, and your own tabs, show here. **✎ Customize** is beside the percentage at the top. **Print**, **PDF** and **Save flight record** are in a card at the bottom, and a line under the progress card jumps you there.
- **⚙ Settings** has a two-part switch: **Preferences** and **Back up & restore**. A small red dot on the tab means a backup is due. The **💾 Back up & restore** link in the footer of every tab takes you straight to the backup half.

The app remembers the tab you used last on that device. Tapping **Customize** in Settings takes you to the checklist so you can edit it. Backing up and restoring work the same from any tab, because they save everything the app has stored on that device.

**Change the place you're checking.** On **Conditions**, tap **Change**, then tap the search box. Your saved places (the last 10) drop down; type to narrow them, or type a town, ZIP code or latitude, longitude and tap **Search**. Names that start with St, St. or Ste (St Paul, St. Louis) also find the "Saint" spelling.

**Change a limit from Conditions.** Tap the small ⚙ in the corner of the **Wind**, **Temperature** or **KP Index** tile. Once you have added a drone, the gears are hidden; tap the blue drone pill and choose **⚙ Edit drones & limits** instead. **Settings** opens at **Limits**; change the numbers, then tap **← Back to Conditions** at the top to return to the same spot.

**Fly more than one drone? Give each its own limits.** Open **Settings → Limits** and tap **＋ Add a drone**. Name it, then set its wind, Kp and cold/heat limits in the boxes below. Your original limits stay as **Default**. Back on **Conditions**, tap the blue drone pill under the place name to pick which drone you're flying today; the tiles and the Best window use that drone's limits. The pill only shows once you've added a drone. Drones are not linked to checklist tabs. To remove one, select it in Settings → Limits and tap **Delete this drone**.

## Back up and restore your setup

**Back it up after you make changes.** Your customized list lives only in that one browser on that one device. If the browser data gets cleared, you switch phones, or you open it somewhere else, your changes are gone and you'd have to rebuild them from scratch. A backup takes about a minute:

1. Tap the **⚙ Settings** tab, then **💾 Back up & restore** (or tap the **💾 Back up & restore** link at the bottom of any tab).
2. Tap **Save file** (or **Copy code** and paste it into a note).
3. Keep the file somewhere you'll find it, like a cloud folder.

If you ever need it back, **Restore from file…** brings your whole setup back in one tap. Get in the habit of backing up every time you change something, not just when you move to a new device. That's what lets you experiment without worrying. After you change your list or settings (including Pilot, Drone and Reg. #), a red dot appears on the **⚙ Settings** tab, and a red reminder with a **Back up now** button appears there (once you press **Done**, if you were customizing) until you back up or dismiss it.

Your checklist setup is stored on your device. If you get a new phone, clear your browser data, or want the same setup on a tablet, you'd have to rebuild it. A backup saves it so you can bring it back, or copy it to another device, in a minute.

**What a backup includes**

- Your tab names and icons, which tabs and items are hidden, the items you added, and the order you set
- Your units, your region (USA or another country) and your saved places
- Optionally, your current progress (the boxes you've ticked), which only restores if it hasn't expired (see Good to know)

It does not include saved flight records. Those are separate files that you keep yourself.

**To back up**

1. Tap the **⚙ Settings** tab, then **💾 Back up & restore** (or tap the **💾 Back up & restore** link at the bottom of any tab).
2. Leave **Include current progress** ticked if you're moving to another device today and want your ticks to come with you. Untick it to save only your setup, which is best for a backup you'll keep.
3. Tap **Save file**. On an iPhone or iPad the Share sheet opens, so you can choose **Save to Files** or send it to yourself. In Chrome or Edge on a computer a Save dialog opens, so you choose the folder. In Safari or Firefox the browser takes over, and the app says **Backup started. If your browser asks, choose where to save it.** The file goes to your Downloads folder unless your browser is set to ask where to save.

On a computer you can also tap **Share…** to send the backup code with AirDrop, Mail or Messages. To restore it, tap **Paste code…**, paste the code into the box and tap **Restore from code**.

Or tap **Copy code**. The code is the same backup as text. Paste it into **Back up & restore** in another browser or app (for example, from Safari into your Home Screen app), or keep it somewhere safe, such as a note.

**To restore**

- **From a file:** tap **Restore from file…** and choose your backup. The page reloads with that setup.
- **From a code:** tap **Paste code…**, paste the code into the box that opens (it says **Paste a backup code here**), then tap **Restore from code**.

**Move your setup to another device**

Back up on the first device, send the file or code to the second (AirDrop, email, a cloud folder or a message to yourself), then restore it there.

**Good to know**

- **Progress expires, even in a backup.** Your ticks are kept for the time set under **Clear saved progress after** (12 hours unless you changed it; on the **Checklist** tab, tap **change** at the end of the "clears after" note to open that setting), counted from the last time you ticked something. If you restore a backup after that time has passed, the app restores your setup, leaves the old ticks out and tells you the progress has expired. So a backup with progress works for moving to another device today, not for storing for later.
- **Restoring replaces everything on that device:** tabs, hidden and added items, units and saved places. If you want to keep what you have, save a backup of it first.
- **Each place keeps its own copy.** Safari and the Home Screen icon on the same iPhone store their settings separately. You can back up from one and restore into the other. That's an easy way to move to the Home Screen app.
- **You can restore on the same device too.** It's a quick way to undo a round of changes you regret, if you saved a backup first.
- **Uncheck all and Restore original list are different.** **Uncheck all** on the main screen clears only your ticks, flags and N/A marks on that tab. **Restore original list**, in the customize panel, undoes your customizing on that tab: hidden items come back, items you added are removed and the tab name and icon are reset.
- **Your files are yours.** They're plain text files that stay on your device or wherever you put them. Nothing is sent to the website.

## Make your own profiles

The app keeps one setup at a time on your device: your tab names and icons, which items are shown, items you added, your units and your saved places. A **backup file** is a snapshot of that setup. Keep one backup file per kind of job, and you can switch between them in a few taps.

**Example: a "Timelapse" setup**

1. Tap **✎ Customize** at the top of the **✅ Checklist** tab (or **⚙ Settings**, then **Customize** in the **Your checklist & tab** row).
2. Rename a tab (for example, **Timelapse**) in the **Tab name** box. You can also change its icon: tap **Change ›** next to **Tab icon**, then pick a drone icon (and a color), type your own emoji, or choose **No icon**.
3. Tap **Hide** on items you don't need, and add your own at the bottom of any section. Use **Hide this tab** on tabs you don't want to see. Tap **✓ Done**.
4. In **💾 Back up & restore**, untick **Include current progress**, then tap **Save file**.
5. Rename the file so you can tell it apart, for example `uas-checklist-backup-TIMELAPSE.json`.

**Tip: add a link to an item.** When you add an item, the optional **Link or app address** box makes it a shortcut that sits under the item's text. Use it for a **Check airspace** item that opens your airspace app, or a number to call. It works for more than websites:

| Type this in the link box | The item shows | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `airaware.aloft.ai` or `https://airaware.aloft.ai` | **Open airaware.aloft.ai ›** | Opens the website. On an iPhone, a site with its own app may open in that app. |
| `mailto:someone@example.com` | **someone@example.com ›** | Starts an email to that address. You can add a subject and message, for example `mailto:someone@example.com?subject=Job%20done&body=Job%20complete`. |
| `tel:+19105551212` | **+19105551212 ›** | Starts a phone call. |
| `sms:+19105551212` | **+19105551212 ›** | Starts a text message. On an iPhone, add `&body=On%20my%20way` to fill in the message. |
| `facetime:someone@example.com` | **someone@example.com ›** | Starts a FaceTime call. |
| `dji://…` or any `name://…` | **Open name app ›** | Opens that app, if it's installed and publishes an address like this. |
| `shortcuts://run-shortcut?name=Open%20Air%20Aware` | **Open Air Aware ›** | Runs an iPhone or iPad Shortcut by name, and shows that name. See the tip below. |

Spaces aren't allowed in a link: write `%20` for a space, `%0A` for a new line and `%26` for an `&` inside text. Unsafe types such as `javascript:` and `file:` are refused. If the box turns red, the address wasn't accepted. The link sits under the item's text, so ticking the item works as before.

**iPhone tip: open an app that has no web address.** Some apps can't be opened with a link. Make a Shortcut that opens the app, then link to the Shortcut:

1. In the **Shortcuts** app, tap **+** and add the **Open App** action. Choose the app, such as Air Aware.
2. Name the shortcut as an action, for example **Open Air Aware**.
3. In the checklist's **Link or app address** box, type `shortcuts://run-shortcut?name=Open%20Air%20Aware`. The name must match the shortcut's name exactly, with `%20` for each space.

The item then shows **Open Air Aware ›**, the shortcut's own name. Tapping it runs the shortcut, which opens your app. The Shortcuts app may flash on screen first, and iOS may ask you to allow it the first time. This works on iPhone and iPad only.

**Tip: one tab for each drone.** If you fly more than one aircraft, make a tab for each. Open **Customize** on the tab you want to set up and tap **Clone another tab onto this one…**. Choose the tab to copy from, read the confirmation (it names both tabs, and the one you're on is the one that gets replaced), and tap **Replace**. Then rename the tab, pick its icon, and type its own **Drone** and **Reg. #** under **For records and PDFs saved from this tab**. **Undo** appears right after, and works until you leave or reload the page. **Restore original list** brings the tab's own list back. Back up afterwards.

**Copy one item to another tab.** Each item you added has a **Copy to…** button in **Customize**. Choose the tab, and the item and its link are copied into the same section. If that tab already has the item, it tells you instead of adding a second one.

**Switch tabs while customizing.** The bar at the bottom of the screen shows the tab you're editing, and small tab buttons on it jump to another tab, so you don't have to scroll up.

**Change or delete an item you added.** In **Customize**, each item you added has a blue **Edit** button and a red **Delete** button on a row under it. **Edit** opens the text and the link in place; **Save** keeps the item's place and checkmark. The **Link or app address** box also suggests links you've already saved, so you can tap one instead of typing it again. Back up afterwards.

### What my Timelapse profile looks like

I shoot repeat timelapses: same spot, same flight path, session after session, so the frames line up when they're stacked together. The standard checklist is built for a one-off job, so I made a version just for this:

- I **renamed** one tab to **Timelapse**.
- I **hid the other two tabs**, so the app opens straight to this checklist.
- I **hid the standard items** on that tab and **added my own**, about 60 of them across the four sections.

Here is a sample of what I put in, to show the idea:

- **Before leaving home:** "Do NOT edit the saved waypoint mission. Create a second mission instead." And: "Check weather: fly at a consistent time of day when possible for matching light."
- **On site, before flight:**
  - "Launch from the EXACT same point every session. Use a physical marker (stake, spray paint, cone)."
  - "White balance: lock manually; must match previous sessions."
  - "Set MANUAL exposure before loading the mission. Record your values each session."
  - "Note the EV setting used on the last session and match it today."
  - "Confirm waypoint count matches previous sessions before arming."
- **During flight:** "Zoomed-in frame: note it for deletion." And: "Wrong lens active: scene noticeably wider or tighter than usual."
- **After:** "Review all images on the controller. Delete bad frames (wrong angle, zoom, very dark) now."

None of this is in the standard list, because it only matters for timelapse. That's why a separate profile works better than adding a dozen items you don't need on every other job.

I did the same thing for my other kinds of work: one backup file each for General, Mapping, Commercial, Timelapse and Production, each showing a single tab. Before a shoot I restore the one I need.

**Make yours:** start from whatever tab is closest to your work. Hide what you never use, add the checks you keep forgetting, and write them in your own words and for your own gear. The best items come from the mistakes you've already made once.

**To switch to it later:** tap the **⚙ Settings** tab, then **💾 Back up & restore**, then **Restore from file…**, and choose the file. The page reloads with that setup.

**Good to know**

- **Restoring replaces everything on that device:** tabs, hidden and added items, units and saved places. If you want to keep your normal setup, save a backup of it first, before you start experimenting.
- **Untick "Include current progress"** when you save a profile. Otherwise it also saves which boxes you had ticked.
- **Where files go and moving between devices:** see [Back up and restore your setup](#back-up-and-restore-your-setup). Put your profile files somewhere you'll find them, such as a cloud folder.
- **Your files are yours.** They're plain text files that stay on your device or wherever you put them. Nothing is sent to the website.

## Use it with no signal

The checklist works with no signal. Once you've opened the app **once with a connection**, it stays ready:

- **Works offline:** the whole checklist, ticking items, your own items and tabs, and your saved settings.
- **Needs a connection:** live weather, radar, KP index, airport reports and place search. With no signal, the weather card shows **📴 No signal** (with your last readings if it has them) and refreshes by itself when you're back online.
- **Updates:** when you do have a signal, the app loads the newest version. With no signal it opens the last copy it loaded.

**Get it ready before a trip**

1. Open the app once while you have a signal (home Wi-Fi is fine).
2. Close it, switch on airplane mode, and open it again to check it loads.

**iPhone and iPad:** a Home Screen icon has its own storage, separate from Safari. If you use the icon, open it once with a signal as well.

Still worth doing: the checklist's own tip to screenshot your mission brief. The app is offline-ready, but your brief, airspace map and NOTAMs aren't.

## Save a flight record

Want a copy of what you checked, plus the details of the flight? Tap **Save flight record**, in the card at the bottom of the checklist, under **Print | PDF**. From the top, tap **↓ Print, PDF and Save flight record are at the bottom** to jump there.

1. Tap **Save flight record**.
2. Fill in what you want to keep: the customer, the site (type an address or place name, or leave it blank) and any notes. **More details** has boxes for airspace authorization (for example a LAANC reference, altitude and times), flight time and visual observer. Everything is optional.
3. Choose whether to include the weather conditions, the items you didn't check, and your GPS coordinates (off unless you tick it).
4. Tap **Save PDF** or **Save text**. The one you used last is the filled button at the left of the row, and the app remembers it. PDF is the filled button until you choose text. On an iPhone or iPad the Share sheet opens, so you can choose **Save to Files**, AirDrop, Mail or Messages. On a computer, **PDF** opens a box with **Download** (saves to your Downloads folder) and **Share…**; text records go to your Downloads folder.

The line above the buttons shows the PDF layout and paper size in use, for example **PDF: Compact · A4**. Tap **(change in Settings)** to go straight to them; what you typed stays in the box.

You get one file, named like `flight-record-2026-10-06-1415-smith-roofing.pdf` (or `.txt` for text), that opens on any device. The PDF has real check boxes and page numbers. Items you flagged are listed as NEEDS ATTENTION. Items you hid are left out and items you added are included. The record is made on your device and isn't sent anywhere. To put your own name at the front of the file, such as `johnston-aerial`, type it in **Record file name starts with** at the bottom of **⚙ Settings**. The date, time and customer are still added.

Want your name and aircraft printed on every record? In **⚙ Settings**, type your name in **Default pilot**, your drone in **Default drone** and its registration number in **Default reg. #**. They are printed near the top of each saved record, and filled in on the checklist **PDF** too. They stay on that device, and **Back up & restore** carries them. Leave a box empty and nothing is printed for it.

**Fly more than one drone?** Give each drone its own tab. Tap **✎ Customize** on the Checklist tab, and under **For records and PDFs saved from this tab** type that tab's **Pilot**, **Drone** and **Reg. #**. A box you leave empty uses the default from Settings. To start a new drone with the same list, use **Clone another tab onto this one…** (see "Make your own profiles"). Back up when you're done.

If you close the Share sheet without choosing anything, the Save record box stays open with what you typed. If you close the box by tapping outside it, what you typed comes back for the next 12 hours. It is cleared once you save or copy the record, or tap **Cancel**.

Prefer to paste it into a note or email? Tap **Copy as text instead**. The record is copied to your clipboard and no file is made. That doesn't change which button is filled.

If the Share sheet doesn't open, the app shows a box with **Share…** and **Download** buttons instead. On a Mac or other computer, a PDF always offers that box first, because the Mac share sheet has no Print or Save to Files. **Download** saves the PDF to your Downloads folder, and you can open it there to print. On an iPad the box has **View** and **Share…** instead, and **Share…** includes **Save to Files**.

## Print it and use a pencil

You can print the checklist and take it to the site. Open the tab you want, scroll to the bottom of the checklist (or tap **↓ Print, PDF and Save flight record are at the bottom**), then tap **Print** in Safari. In the Home Screen app, open the page in Safari first, since the Home Screen app can't print. Check items off with a pencil during preflight and again after the flight. Print each tab you need, since only the open tab prints.

**Print** uses your browser's page settings, so paper size and margins come from its print dialog. **Print** is always one column in large type, with every section open. It has no layout choice. If you want it compact, tap **PDF**, choose **Compact** in **⚙ Settings**, and print the PDF. For a different paper, tap **PDF** instead. Using the Home Screen app, or no printer? Tap **PDF** as well. It makes the same sheet as a PDF file, with your checked and flagged items marked and lines for date, time, site, pilot and visual observer, that you can save to Files, email or print later. It works in the Home Screen app too, and like printing it covers only the open tab.

**Too much small print in Settings?** Tap **Hide explanations** at the top of **Preferences** (or of **Back up & restore**) to fold away the grey explanation text. Tap **ⓘ Show explanations** to bring it back. The app remembers your choice on that device, and it is saved with your backup. The note that your settings are saved on this device only always stays.

**Choose how the PDF looks.** Open **⚙ Settings** and look for **PDF layout** and **PDF paper** near the bottom. **Compact** puts the items in two columns at a slightly smaller size, so a full checklist takes about half the pages (the General list is 2 pages instead of 4 on Letter). **Large print** keeps the bigger, one-column layout. **Letter** and **A4** pick the paper size. The first time, the app chooses Letter for English and North American browsers and A4 elsewhere, and then remembers your choice. It applies to the checklist **PDF** and to the PDF in a saved flight record, and **Back up & restore** carries it with your other settings. After you save a PDF, the message tells you which layout and paper it used.

The app also works offline once it has loaded (see above), so paper is a choice, not a requirement. You can also save a flight record afterward and print that.

## Use it outside the USA

The first time you open the app it asks **Where do you fly?** Choose **Outside USA**. You can change this at any time from **Where you fly** under **⚙ Settings**.

**What changes**

- **Units:** distances show in km, temperatures in °C. The **Imperial / Metric** button next to the weather title switches them without changing your region. Wind starts as km/h; tap the unit on the Wind tile to change it (mph, km/h, m/s or knots).
- **Checklist wording:** the items marked "(US)" are hidden: the TFR check, the LAANC item and the TFR recheck on site. The NOTAM item is reworded to point you to your country's aviation authority or NOTAM service.
- **Ceiling warnings:** low-cloud notes say "check your local cloud and visibility rules" instead of quoting US rules.
- **Night notice:** after sunset or before sunrise it says to check your local rules for night flying. The same line is printed on a saved flight record.
- **Place search:** you can search by town, postal code (ZIP code in the USA), or enter coordinates. Add the country to search outside the USA, for example **Munich, Germany**.

**What works worldwide**

- Forecast weather, wind, visibility, dew point, sunrise/sunset times, golden hour windows and the best window of the next 12 daylight hours (or the next 12 hours, if you turn on **I fly at night** under **⚙ Settings**).
- The **Next 24 hours** strip under the tiles: swipe sideways to see each hour, and tap the small chip on its title to switch between Wind, Aloft, Temp, Visibility, Clouds and Rain. Tap **10 days** beside it for a day-by-day outlook (lighter days are a rough guide). The place name the forecast is for is shown beside the switch, so you can check it without scrolling to the top. Your choices are remembered on this device.
- On the Wind and Cloud Ceiling tiles, other airports within 30 miles can appear one per line: **Windier nearby** (only when their wind is outside your green band), **Lower nearby** and **Also reporting**. Obstructions and terrain can make it windier where you fly than any report shows.
- On the Cloud Ceiling tile, **Model for your spot** shows the forecast cloud cover and low cloud for your location, as a guide next to the airport report.
- On the Temperature tile, **Elevation** is how high your spot is, and **Density alt** estimates how thin the air acts at that temperature, humidity and pressure. Hot, humid days and high places push it up. When it is well above the elevation (bold), expect less lift and shorter flights.
- **Keep screen on** (under **⚙ Settings**, default No): choose Yes to stop the screen locking while the app is open and in view. It uses more battery. If your phone or browser can't do this, the setting says so.
- Radar, wherever RainViewer has coverage (the card tells you where it doesn't).
- Airport reports (METAR) from airports near you.
- The KP index.

**Buttons under the weather, by country:**

- **The buttons follow the place you look up**, not just your **Where you fly** setting. When you search for a place and pick it, the app remembers its country. A US pilot who looks up a site in Australia gets Australia's button, and a pilot outside the USA who looks up a US site gets the US ones.
- **🛩 Airspace chart — SkyVector** is always the top-left button. It has charts for many countries (its TFR overlay is US only, and it knows nothing about your country's drone rules).
- **In the USA:** **FAA UAS Facility Map**, **TFRs — FAA.gov** and **NOTAMs — AIM** sit beside and below it.
- **Countries with their own button:** **🇦🇺 Drone safety apps — CASA** (Australia, the regulator's list of verified drone safety apps), **🇦🇷 Drones — ANAC** (Argentina, rules and registration), **🇫🇷 Drones — DGAC** (France, the government's open-category drone page, with the restrictions map, registration and rules), **🇧🇪 Drone map — Droneguide** (Belgium, the official national map), **🇬🇧 Drone map — NATS** (the UK, the map the CAA points to) and **🇨🇦 Drone map — NRC** (Canada, the National Research Council's drone site selection tool). Since v4.1.2 there are also buttons for **🇦🇹 Austria** (Dronespace), **🇨🇭 Switzerland** (FOCA map), **🇮🇹 Italy** (D-Flight), **🇪🇸 Spain** (ENAIRE), **🇩🇰 Denmark** (dronezoner), **🇸🇪 Sweden** (LFV), **🇳🇴 Norway** (Avinor), **🇫🇮 Finland** (Traficom), **🇵🇱 Poland** (DroneRadar), **🇨🇿 Czech Republic** (DroneMap), **🇳🇱 Netherlands** (GoDrone), **🇩🇪 Germany** (dipul) and **🇮🇪 Ireland** (IAA). Some of these sites open in the local language. In these countries the FAA buttons are hidden, because they don't apply there.
- **Any other country:** with **Outside USA** chosen, or a place picked from search in another country, you get **🌍 Find your aviation authority**, a list of national aviation authorities with their websites, and a line asking you to tell us which country to add. GPS and typed coordinates carry no country, so for them the app goes by the time zone and your setting.
- These buttons are links to official pages. Pages can move, and none of them replaces the rules in your own country, so check before you fly.

**An honest limit:** this is a guide, not legal advice. The app doesn't know your country's drone rules. Check with your own aviation authority, such as Transport Canada, EASA or your national CAA, before you fly. Please also tell us which items don't fit where you fly. That feedback shapes the next version.

## Check TFRs and NOTAMs before you fly (USA)

**This section is for flying in the USA only.** TFRs and NOTAMs here are the FAA's. In countries outside the USA the **FAA UAS Facility Map**, **TFRs** and **NOTAMs** buttons are hidden, so use your own country's aviation authority and drone airspace app instead (see [Use it outside the USA](#use-it-outside-the-usa)).

### TFRs

The **⚠️ TFRs** button shows a count under its name, such as **NC: 3 · US: 12**: how many TFRs the FAA lists for your state, then how many are nationwide. It is a count only. It does not say whether one is near you, so open the list and check. The button opens the FAA's list for the whole country, so most of what you see will be far from you. To narrow it down:

1. In the left sidebar, open **Please select a state** and pick your state. **On a phone, turn it sideways first.** The page then shows its search boxes down the left side. Tap the **second box** (search by state), scroll to your state and pick it. That is what makes the list usable on a phone.
2. If nothing is listed, there are no TFRs in your state right now. If you fly near a border, check the neighboring state too.
3. Tap the **magnifier** icon next to an entry to see it on the map.
4. Read the **Type** column. VIP, Security, and Hazards (fires, for example) are the ones that matter for flying.

**Use a bigger view.** Both FAA sites (TFRs and NOTAMs) are built for a computer screen. If a link opens inside the app, open it in your browser instead. On iPhone, tap the compass icon. On Android, look for **Open in browser** (or **Open in Chrome**) in the menu. Then turn your phone sideways, since landscape gives the list and the map much more room.

**Easier on a phone: the Airspace chart button.** The **🛩 Airspace chart — SkyVector** button under the weather (always top left) opens a free aviation chart centered on your spot, and TFRs are drawn on it. Tap a TFR to open the FAA's details page for it. It works on phones and tablets without signing in. Two cautions: it is a pilot's sectional chart, so it is busy and not drone-specific, and it does not show every TFR the FAA lists (some, such as launch and security TFRs, can be missing). Use it for a quick look and still confirm against the FAA's TFR list above.

**Easier on a phone: an FAA-approved airspace app.** The FAA's TFR list is built for a desktop screen. The FAA's B4UFLY service is delivered through FAA-approved apps from private companies, and these show airspace advisories, including TFRs, on a map around you. That's much easier to read at the launch site. Treat the map as a quick look, and still confirm against the FAA's current TFR and NOTAM pages, because an app can lag behind or miss a notice.

### FAA UAS Facility Map (the LAANC grid)

The **🗺 FAA UAS Facility Map** button opens the FAA's UAS Facility Map, centered on the place you chose. Near airports in controlled airspace the map is split into grids, and each grid shows the highest altitude the FAA can approve automatically through LAANC. A grid showing 0 is not available for automatic approval.

The button also reads the grid around your place and shows it on the button. It looks at every grid square within 3 miles, not just one, because the ceilings change from square to square near an airport. You'll see a range such as **0–400 ft nearby · LAANC** (amber, because some nearby squares are 0 ft and need FAA approval), a single number such as **400 ft · LAANC** (every nearby square matches and LAANC is available), **100 ft · no LAANC**, **0 ft · FAA approval** (every nearby square needs authorization) or **No grid nearby** (the FAA has no grid within 3 miles, which is common away from controlled airports). A city search uses the middle of town, so for the exact square, use **My location** or tap the button and look at the map. If the FAA's data service can't be reached, the button still opens the map but shows no line. This is a guide only. It does not show TFRs, and it does not replace LAANC or your airspace app, so check those before you fly.

### Reading NOTAMs

Like the TFR page, the NOTAM search is easier to use in your browser with your phone turned sideways.

The **📋 NOTAMs** button opens the FAA's NOTAM search, which starts with an empty **Location** box. To save typing, the button shows the nearest airport code (for example **KRDU**) and copies it when you tap it. Open the FAA page, paste into **Location** and press **Search**.

Want a different airport? Under the buttons, the **Nearby** row lists up to three airports with their distances. Tap one to switch the button to it, and the code is copied. Tap **All 3** to copy all three, comma-separated, and search them together. Only airports that report weather appear in the list, so for a small field that isn't there, type its code yourself.

A few tips make the NOTAM search much less noisy:

1. **Search by airport code, not coordinates.** A coordinate search also returns center-wide notices for your whole region (for central NC they show as "ZDC"). Enter the codes of your nearest airports instead (the **Nearby** row has them), plus any airport near your job site.
2. **Use the free-text search** to find notices aimed at you. Try **UAS** or **drone**.
3. **Check the dates first.** Look at the start and end times before anything else, since many notices are expired or haven't started yet. Times are in UTC (Zulu), so convert them to your local time. For example, 1200Z is 8 AM Eastern daylight time.
4. **Know which notices matter to a small drone:**
   - Anything that starts with **FLIGHT RESTRICTIONS**. That's a TFR.
   - Anything mentioning **UAS** or **drones**.
   - **Obstructions** (OBST), such as cranes and towers, near your site.
   - **Airspace activity** such as parachute jumping, airshows, and military activity.
   - You can skip runway, taxiway, and navigation-aid notices. They matter to pilots at the airport, not to you.
5. **Learn the shorthand as you go.** SFC is ground level, AGL is above ground level, WI is within, and NM is nautical miles. "WI 3NM … SFC-400FT AGL" means within 3 nautical miles, from the ground up to 400 feet, which overlaps normal drone altitude.

A NOTAM search doesn't replace your airspace check (LAANC or your drone airspace app). It tells you about temporary changes. Always confirm against the FAA's current pages.
